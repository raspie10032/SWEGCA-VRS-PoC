## Title

Evidence-Gated Experience and Verified Reinforcement Synapses across Context Resets and Replaceable Reasoning Models

## Authors

Dongjun Park

Affiliation in manuscript: Independent Researcher

## Abstract

We present a reference implementation of SWEGCA with Verified Reinforcement Synapses (VRS). A main process owns persistent cognitive state, experience, plastic relations and authority; replaceable models produce proposals. We ask whether VRS intervention changes measured output, regardless of correctness. The endpoint is a memory-branch decision, not a final main-level semantic commit. Activation uses familiarity, recall, replay and re-evidence with VRS-dependent evidence promotion. After an E2B context reset, provenance-bound refutations survived in 3/3 requests. Replacing E2B with a fixed 135.5M specialist and GPT-5.6 Luna retained the main-state pair in six calls on the same three requests, with refutation or abstention. Exact verdict retention was 3/6, all from Luna. Five historical episodes remained retrievable from a later full-current snapshot. These are bounded observations, not proof of general cognitive continuity, comprehensive safety or equal specialist capability. In a three-source diagnostic, current VRS changed one memory-branch decision relative to frozen and disabled VRS under identical recalled candidates; two were unchanged. An earlier twelve-source A/B task had no full-system versus frozen-VRS choice changes. Disabled VRS mechanically prevents promotion, limiting relation-specific interpretation. Two historical 100-source evaluations offer secondary analogy and utility data, not isolated VRS evidence: accuracy was 0.64 in one and 0.60 versus a current-evidence baseline of 0.82 in the other. Their hypotheses are not re-evidence-approved judgments. We separate task-specific results and original gates from post-hoc interpretation. This concept-validation study provides an executable reference path and one observed VRS-dependent output difference, not evidence of general benefit, a population effect, cognitive growth or a finished product.
